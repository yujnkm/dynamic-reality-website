## Facilities

The Dynamic Reality Lab is a 1,200-square-foot facility in the newly renovated Nebraska Hall (NH W192), built for controlled XR research and experimentation. The space is divided into four areas: a conference room, a data center (W192C) behind its own secure door, student offices, and a main research area. Every room except the data center has west-facing windows that bring in natural light throughout the day. The Engineering Complex quad right next door is our main outdoor area for wide-area augmented reality experiments, and the lab also has flight simulator facilities, described below.

- **Augmented reality:** Two Magic Leap 2, four Microsoft HoloLens 2, and Snapchat Spectacles.
- **Video passthrough and VR:** Ten Meta Quest 3, two Meta Quest Pro, and one Apple Vision Pro.
- **Fabrication:** Two Bambu Lab 3D printers and printed circuit board (PCB) fabrication equipment.
- **Spatial capture and reconstruction:** An XGRIDS PortalCam scanner, a Ricoh THETA 360° camera, and dedicated LiDAR cameras.
- **Student workspace (W192):** Six temporary workstations with Alienware desktops and Quest 3 headsets for rapid prototyping.
- **DRL member workspace (W192A):** Assigned seating for PhD and master's students.
- **GPU workstations (W192C):** Ten Intel Core Ultra 9 PCs with NVIDIA GPUs: two RTX A6000 (48 GB), two RTX 4080 SUPER (16 GB), and six RTX 5070 Ti (16 GB).

Our lab custom-builds a headset that combines a Magic Leap 2 with a 14-channel EEG system, so researchers can record brain activity while users work in our augmented reality systems, such as AR Cockpit. In partnership with the **NIMBUS** Lab, we pair the headset with an FAA-approved Noble Flight Simulation (NFS) Cirrus Commercial Cockpit. AR cockpit overlays let us study pilot performance and human–machine interaction, helping shape the future of flight.

### Lab History

The Dynamic Reality Lab started in early 2024, taking shape at UC Santa Barbara before opening its first physical space at Texas A&M University. In January 2026 the lab moved to its permanent home at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln, where it continues to run wide-area outdoor AR experiments and study human-AI interaction in mixed reality.

### Prospective PhD Students

Every PhD student here is fully funded. Under Dr. You-Jin Kim’s supervision, students receive a **$2,800** monthly stipend year-round, including summers, plus full health insurance and tuition remission at in-state and out-of-state rates.

For local context, a one-bedroom loft in downtown Lincoln runs about $1,090 per month as of April 2026. Our students can live comfortably on the stipend and keep their attention on the research.

### Undergraduate and Current UNL Students {#unl-students}

We regularly bring UNL students onto active projects. The same three steps apply: start with the interest form, then contact any lab member whose work interests you.

- **Research is not coursework.** There is no syllabus and no set of expected answers. You develop your own direction, with guidance from a mentor.
- **Classes are still a good entry point.** Spatial Interaction and Simulation, Generative AI and Design, Computer Graphics, Wide-Area AR + Interactive Narrative, or Advanced Topics in Software Engineering all lead somewhere.
- **Do a little homework on us.** Read our recent publications, see what the PhD students are working on, then contact those whose work interests you.
- **Be specific.** Tell us what you want to work on, which project, and roughly how many hours a week you can commit.

{% endcapture %}
